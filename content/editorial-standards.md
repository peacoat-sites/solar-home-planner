# How Solar Home Planner Is Written

Solar Home Planner follows a residential solar project from first quote to a working system: sizing, equipment, batteries, permits and payback. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Solar Home Planner are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a licensed electrician or installer before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Solar Home Planner (David Torres, Morgan Johnson, Rachel Kim, Stephanie Walsh, Tom Bradley) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [U.S. Dept. of Energy: Solar](https://www.energy.gov/eere/solar/homeowners-guide-going-solar): Homeowner solar guidance
- [National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)](https://www.nrel.gov/): Solar research and data
- [DSIRE: Incentives Database](https://www.dsireusa.org/): State solar incentives
- [ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/): Efficiency and rebates

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Solar and battery systems involve high-voltage wiring and a connection to the grid, and they usually need a permit, an inspection and utility approval. Leave the electrical work to a licensed electrician or installer unless your jurisdiction allows owner installation and you are qualified to do it. Production and payback figures on this site are estimates that depend on your roof, shading, usage and utility rates.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Solar Home Planner may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
