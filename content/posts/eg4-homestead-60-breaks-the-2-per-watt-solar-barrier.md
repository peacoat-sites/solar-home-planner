EG4 dropped a number that the off-grid and rural solar crowd has been waiting years to see: $1.20 per watt for a complete solar array. Not a panel cost. Not a wholesale quote with a dozen asterisks. The all-in price for 14.4 kilowatts DC of mounted, prewired solar, as part of the new Homestead 60 system unveiled November 17, 2026. That figure breaks the $2-per-watt barrier that has quietly defined the ceiling of DIY and installer ambitions for residential solar for the better part of a decade.

The Homestead 60 pairs that 14.4 kW of solar (24 x 600 W modules across four ground-mount racks) with 60 kWh of usable LFP [battery storage](/diy-solar-with-battery-storage/) using CATL cells, all in a preassembled, prewired package. The MAP price is $37,697, which works out to $2.62 per watt all-in for solar plus storage combined. The companion battery, the EG4 XR 60, can be purchased separately starting at $20,499, which EG4 says puts it at $342 per usable kilowatt-hour. That would make it the first all-in-one residential storage system to come in under $350/kWh, and it arrives at a moment when storage is no longer the add-on sale. It's the reason people are calling installers in the first place.

## Why the $2-Per-Watt Number Actually Mattered

The $2-per-watt figure wasn't arbitrary. It became the informal benchmark where [DIY solar](/diy-solar-kit-recommendations/) started making obvious financial sense against installer quotes, particularly for rural properties, agricultural operations, and homeowners who couldn't get a competitive installer bid because they were too far out or too small a job. Getting below it for solar equipment alone was possible if you sourced panels aggressively. Getting below it on a complete, warranted, plug-and-play system was not. That gap kept a lot of projects on the drawing board.

EG4 is a Texas-based brand distributed primarily through Signature Solar, and they've built their reputation on aggressive pricing in the DIY channel. The Homestead 60 is a logical extension of that strategy, but the scale of the price move is significant. When EnergySage's mid-2026 data shows average installed residential battery costs running around $15,647 for just 13.5 kWh, the Homestead 60's 60 kWh at $342/kWh is a genuinely different category of offer. You're not comparing apples to apples, but the gap is wide enough that it reshapes the conversation about what large-capacity storage [actually costs](/going-solar-without-the-30-tax-credit-what-it-actually-costs-now/).

## The Storage-First Shift Is Real, and This Timing Isn't Accidental

Battery attachment rates in new residential solar installs hit 45% nationally in Q1 2026, according to TechTimes reporting on U.S. solar installation milestones. In 2022, that number was 12%. California is already at 94% following the NEM 3.0 restructuring, which gutted the economics of exporting power to the grid and made self-consumption the only reliable financial model. Other states are watching California's trajectory and adjusting their own net metering structures.

This is the market context EG4 is reading correctly. Declining export rates mean a battery isn't a nice-to-have anymore if you're trying to make the math work. Storage-first buying decisions are now the dominant pattern post-ITC, as pv magazine USA noted in early November 2026. A system designed around 60 kWh of storage with solar sized to fill it, sold as a single preassembled unit, fits that model precisely.

## Permits, Interconnection, and What This Isn't

A preassembled, prewired ground-mount system still needs permits. That means a site plan, electrical drawings, and in most jurisdictions a licensed electrician or solar contractor to sign off. Ground mounts can also trigger different zoning rules than rooftop installs, particularly in HOA communities or rural counties with agricultural land restrictions. Check your county's development code before you order.

Interconnection with the utility is a separate process regardless of how prebuilt the system is. If you're going grid-tied, you'll need to file an interconnection application, and depending on your utility's queue, that can add weeks or months. If you're going off-grid or backup-only, that step disappears, which is part of why rural and agricultural buyers are the obvious first market for the Homestead 60.

The prewired description means the internal connections within each rack and battery enclosure are factory-done. It does not mean you unbox it and flip a switch. You're still making DC and AC connections at the site level. Know what you're buying.

## Red Flags to Watch Before You Order

EG4 sells through Signature Solar, which has an established reputation in the DIY channel. That said, any high-demand product launch carries supply chain risk. Ask for confirmed lead times in writing before you pay. Get clarity on warranty terms: who handles a warranty claim, EG4 directly or through the distributor, and what the turnaround looks like for a 60 kWh battery that's not performing.

Also verify that your installer or electrician has worked with EG4 equipment before. The brand is well-documented in DIY forums and has solid technical support, but a contractor unfamiliar with the system will cost you time and money sorting out configuration questions. This is a large system. A sizing mistake at 60 kWh is a bigger problem than one at 10 kWh.

The Homestead 60 is a real milestone, not just a marketing number. At $1.20 per watt for solar and $342 per usable kilowatt-hour for storage, it sets a new reference point that will pressure competitors and give serious DIY and rural buyers a credible option at a scale that actually moves the needle on energy independence. Do your permitting homework, get your interconnection paperwork started early, and get the lead time confirmed in writing. The price is right. The rest is on you to execute correctly.
